# Family Expense Tracker

**Product requirements · v1 · 8 November 2026**  
**Working name:** Family Expense Tracker. **Owner and developer:** solo founder. **Market:** India. **Platform:** Android. **Companion:** [Technical build plan](buildplan.md).

## 1. Product and customer

Two adults pay from different phones and accounts. One person usually reconciles the household total from messages, statements, or spreadsheets. The app captures supported payment alerts automatically, keeps each adult’s feed private, and produces a shared household ledger from expenses each adult chooses to contribute. The weekly review should answer what was spent together, what needs checking, and which sources are represented.

**First customer:** two adults already coordinating everyday household spending. A household can grow to five adult accounts. An adult may belong to one household at a time. Dependents appear as optional spending labels such as “School” or “Parents”; no child accounts, names of children, birth dates, or child-level profiles are collected. No bank partnerships, payment initiation, account balances, or bank credentials.

**Market baseline:** FinArt already offers automatic capture and family budgets; Splitkaro offers SMS capture, shared bills, and settlements; Fold offers family totals via Spaces; GharHisab advertises private and family spaces on its waitlist. A one-time price and presets help explain the offer. The testable advantage is faster correction and a more explainable shared total for real households. Public complaints about missed formats and duplicated household bookkeeping are directional. Use a paired, statement-based pilot to measure the gap. ## 2. Where v1 should outperform rivals

These are hypotheses with acceptance checks. Several competitors already offer parts of this workflow.

| Opportunity | What v1 does | What we measure |
|---|---|---|
| Trust the household total | Explain the source of each detected payment in the private feed. Show each adult their account and card coverage, permission failures, last capture, and statement-checked periods. The shared review shows contributing adults and only the records they shared; it never exposes their private account aliases or source activity. Mark unverified coverage explicitly. | Missed and duplicate payments against statements; whether both adults can identify an unverified period without help. |
| Spend less time correcting automation | Group detected payments by merchant or category. A merchant correction can apply to selected past matches, future matches, or both. Preview the match count and allow undo. Past changes stay local unless the payer separately approves changes to already-shared entries. | Time and taps to correct five payments from the same merchant; whether a future payment follows the rule; zero silent shared changes. |
| Share expenses without turning them into debts | Keep a private feed per adult. Preview exact sharing rules for a merchant or dedicated household card, and show payer and beneficiary on shared items. Use a shared household total without opening a settlement balance. | Whether both adults can share school fees or groceries while leaving a private purchase unshared; count accidental disclosures. |
| Replace spreadsheet month-end work | Weekly review and month-end check bring together shared spending, budget remaining, likely duplicates, unresolved items and statement-coverage status. Flag a credit-card repayment or own-account transfer separately from spending. | Time to verify the household total, remaining error in rupees, and whether households still copy totals into a spreadsheet. |
| Make setup and purchase easy to understand | Start with useful life-stage and work categories without collecting exact age or profession. Offer a single household purchase, on-device alert parsing, and structured export. | Time to first useful shared total, willingness to grant SMS access through the actual installer, and willingness to pay after both adults try the workflow. |

Treat fewer mistakes, faster review, and privacy controls as the reasons to buy. Presets and one-time pricing support the offer. Neither establishes a competitive lead on its own.

### Categories and budgets

- Base categories: groceries, housing, utilities, transport, healthcare, eating out, shopping, education, family support and other. Uncategorised is a separate review state. These start with stable seed codes. A household can add, rename and archive shared categories; archive preserves history. Shared categories and personal categories are separate. At share time suggest the matching household seed or explicitly map the category.
- Optional packs: school-age household (fees, uniforms, tuition, school transport); student (hostel, books, courses); salaried (commute, work meals, training); self-employed (supplies, software, business travel); caregiving/retirement (medicines, checkups, caregiving). Multiple packs allowed. Keep selected packs in local settings. Store user-approved shared categories only.
- Budget values are optional integer paise per category per calendar month. Owner sets shared budgets, members set private budgets. Alerts at 80% and 100% once per category, month and threshold. No carry-over or projected balances. Local notification text hides amounts and merchants by default.

### Accounting and coverage

- Confirmed purchases and qualifying external outgoing payments increase gross spending. Incoming money, card repayments, self-transfers, wallet top-ups, ATM withdrawals, failed and pending events are excluded. Present withdrawals separately. Cash without a digital record is outside coverage.
- A linked refund reduces spending in the **month the refund posts**. Display gross, refunds and net separately, even when net is negative. A reversal cancels the original purchase once the link is confirmed. Unmatched credits stay out of spending totals. Count a confirmed payment once, regardless of how many alerts describe it.
- A household report includes only shared entries. A linked refund or reversal of a shared purchase inherits the original visibility only after the original owner confirms sharing it. Until then show a private review prompt. Shared reversal updates that purchase to excluded/reversed. A recipient cannot infer private counterparties or account aliases from a shared record.
- A source status means the phone observed an alert or statement row. A statement check is marked complete only for the user-confirmed account, period and imported rows. Do not display zero spending as verified merely because no alerts arrived.

### Data ownership, deletion and support

- A payer owns their shared transaction and can withdraw it. Leaving or removal withdraws that member’s shared transactions and revokes their access to the household. Totals can change; confirm this before proceeding. Their personal ledger stays on their device. Owner transfers ownership to leave while others remain, or closes the household. The invite warning explains the effect on history.
- Account deletion revokes sessions and device access, withdraws shared transactions, and erases profile/invites according to the build plan. Local deletion wipes the initiating phone immediately after the server accepts the request. Other devices clear shared data on reconnect or when their bounded offline access expires; previously exported files remain with their holders. Provide web sign-in and an email contact for access/deletion requests if the phone is lost.
- Retain shared transaction detail for 24 months on a rolling basis. Local personal history remains until the user deletes it or uninstalls. Show an export reminder before cloud expiry. Keep the billing references required for disputes and applicable records separately. Never ask customers to email raw SMS or statements; accept optional locally redacted parser fixtures after preview.
- Lost-phone replacement invalidates the old device’s sync immediately. A new phone restores shared records and can rescan available SMS. Private categories, rules and history stay on the original device. CSV export provides a portable record; it is not a full app backup.

## 5. Pilot and public release gates

1. **Installation:** 12 volunteers, ideally six real households. Install through Google Play testing tracks on at least three Android manufacturers and Android 13–16 where available. Require at least 10 of 12 volunteers to complete install and live SMS activation without developer tools, with no unresolved failure in any advertised combination. Verify an update preserves app data and the app shows the correct permission status after a revoke. Expand the supported list only with evidence.
2. **Accuracy:** Compare with statements on supported accounts. Target at least 100 eligible posted payments across both initial banks, minimum 30 per bank. Require at least 95% capture of eligible payments for each advertised bank and payment-type combination with at least 20 examples, 99% correct amount/date among captured payments, and no confirmed duplicate totals in the audited pilot. Track transactions with no alert separately. A payment type without enough evidence stays unadvertised.
3. **Household value:** Compare the same week against the household’s usual workflow and FinArt/Splitkaro where the volunteers consent. At least four of six pairs have both adults contribute shared transactions and use the weekly review, and those pairs can check their totals in under five minutes. Use the benchmark protocol in the build plan. Record whether at least three pairs say they would pay ₹499. Keep the paywall closed if these gates fail; revise and rerun the pilot. Publish comparative claims only for tasks and bank/device combinations actually tested.
4. **Security:** A copied APK cannot activate with an unentitled account. Verify forged callbacks, refund/revocation, old-device access, session renewal, tenant isolation, accidental sharing, delete/export and offline grace. Zero critical/high defects or confirmed cross-household disclosure. Recheck the privacy notice against the release build.
5. **Commerce and readiness:** Complete the appropriate Play Console account, identity/merchant setup and required Play review/testing. Verify Google OAuth with the production Play app-signing fingerprint. Test Play purchase, pending/cancelled outcomes, acknowledgement, restore, duplicate delivery and refund/revocation end to end; perform a controlled real purchase/refund smoke test before public sales. Verify that changing the current Play price does not invalidate an earlier purchase or demand payment again. Verify purchase recovery and backup restore before enabling public sales.
